Foundational Design Principles for AI-Native Systems

These principles represent the foundational beliefs that guide the design of AI-native systems. While technologies, architectures, and implementation patterns will evolve, these principles are likely endure.

01

Outcome

Start with the outcome, not the technology.

Intent

AI is a means to achieve an outcome, not the objective itself. Every design decision should begin with the capability or business outcome you want to create.

Design Test

Remove the proposed technology from the discussion. Can the team still state whose outcome matters, what must change for them, which constraints matter, and what evidence would demonstrate success? If the outcome can only be described in terms of deploying or using the technology, the test fails.

02

Users

Treat humans and AI agents as first-class users.

Intent

AI systems should be designed to serve both humans and AI agents as equal consumers of capabilities, information, and interfaces.

Design Test

Give a representative human and AI agent access to the capability through their intended interfaces. Can each discover what it does, understand its constraints, and complete a representative task without undocumented assistance? If either depends on hidden conventions or a privileged explanation, the test fails.

03

Simplicity

Simplify the path to outcomes.

Intent

Reduce friction by minimizing the effort, decisions, and complexity required to achieve the desired outcome.

Design Test

Map every step, decision, handoff, and configuration choice between the user's starting point and the intended outcome. Can each be justified by value, necessary control, understanding, or recoverability? If something can be removed, combined, defaulted, or automated without materially weakening those qualities, the path is not yet simple enough.

04

Autonomy

Design for appropriate autonomy.

Intent

Deliberately determine which decisions and actions belong to humans, AI agents, or a combination of both based on the nature and consequences of the work.

Design Test

Select a consequential decision or action. Can the design justify who decides, who acts, when approval or escalation is required, and how the action can be stopped or reversed based on consequence, reversibility, risk, and uncertainty? If autonomy is assigned primarily because the technology can perform the action, the test fails.

05

Context

Enable informed decisions and actions.

Intent

Ensure humans and AI agents have the knowledge, context, capabilities, and constraints required to make sound decisions and take effective action.

Design Test

Select a representative consequential decision. Can the responsible human or AI agent identify the relevant sources, assumptions, constraints, permissions, uncertainties, and missing context, and explain why the proposed action follows from them? If critical context is inaccessible or unsupported assumptions remain hidden, the test fails.

Parking Lot

The following ideas are important but have not yet earned a place as foundational design principles:

  • Design for continuous adaptation.
  • Separate capabilities from implementations.
  • Design to learn continuously.

These are candidates for future review. They are not part of the approved principle set.
